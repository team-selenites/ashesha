Stories from the Sky · with Selene

Stories written in the stars

Some patterns in the night sky became stories long before we understood what they really were. Come closer: I'll show you where mythology meets astronomy.

01 · Galaxy

Andromeda Galaxy

About 2.5 million light-years away

The Andromeda Galaxy stretches roughly 220,000 light-years across and lies about 2.5 million light-years from Earth, making it the closest large galaxy to our own. In Greek mythology, Andromeda was a princess chained to a rock as a sacrifice to a sea monster, until the hero Perseus arrived to save her. When I look towards her galaxy, I see ancient light travelling across the darkness, carrying a story that began millions of years ago.

02 · Star cluster

Pleiades

A young cluster, around 100 million years old

The Pleiades are a cluster of young, hot stars, born together from the same cloud of gas and dust around 100 million years ago. In Greek mythology, they were the seven daughters of the Titan Atlas and the sea-nymph Pleione, placed among the stars by Zeus to protect them from the hunter Orion. Look closely, and you may see only six: legend says the seventh sister, Merope, hides her light because she married a mortal.

03 · Nebula

Orion Nebula

About 1,300 light-years away

About 1,300 light-years away, the Orion Nebula is a vast stellar nursery where thousands of stars are being born. The ancient Greeks saw a mighty hunter in this region of the sky: Orion, with his sword hanging from his belt. I like to imagine that the glowing cloud beneath his belt is a little piece of celestial magic, where new stars are born beneath a legendary hunter's watch.

04 · Globular cluster

Hercules Globular Cluster

Also known as M13 · Around 25,000 light-years away

The Hercules Globular Cluster, also known as M13, is a dazzling ball of more than 100,000 stars, packed together about 25,000 light-years from Earth. It lies in the constellation Hercules, named after Heracles, the Greek hero whose twelve legendary labours tested his strength and courage. Even heroes deserve a place among the stars, and I think this glittering cluster makes a magnificent home for his legend.

05 · Supernova remnant

Crab Nebula

The supernova was observed in 1054

The Crab Nebula is the expanding remnant of a supernova first observed in 1054, whose brilliant light was recorded by astronomers in ancient China and Japan. Its name came centuries later, when astronomers noticed that its tangled filaments resembled a crab. At its heart spins a tiny, powerful neutron star, beating like a cosmic heart about thirty times every second.

06 · Nebula

Swan Nebula

Also called the Omega Nebula · About 5,500 light-years away

The Swan Nebula, also called the Omega Nebula, is a vast cloud of glowing gas and dust about 5,500 light-years away, where new stars are being born. Its graceful shape has reminded skywatchers of a swan, with a long curved neck gliding through the darkness. In Greek mythology, Zeus once took the form of a swan to approach Leda, and I like to imagine that his celestial disguise still glides among the stars.

These stories bring together scientific facts and ancient mythology. The myths are cultural stories, not scientific explanations of the objects.
