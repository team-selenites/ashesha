The first light on your day

Four short steps with Selene: tell her your birthday, watch the ring of sky SPHEREx photographed on that date, meet the famous objects it caught, and keep a card of it.

Why The First Light? When a new telescope takes its very first picture, astronomers call it first light. Here, the first light is yours: the sky SPHEREx saw on your birthday.

SPHEREx began its survey in 2025, so this is the sky on your birthday in 2025 or 2026, not the year you were born

Why a ring?

SPHEREx circles the Earth from pole to pole, about 14 times a day, always with its back to the Sun so its cold telescope stays cold. It can only look sideways, roughly 90 degrees from the Sun. As it circles, that sideways view sweeps a ring across the sky.

As the Earth goes round the Sun, the ring turns a little each day. After six months it has swept the whole sky. Our Track D check found that the same date a year later shows the same part of the sky, but not the same pictures.
